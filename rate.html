Letters

Domestic

Three things could impact the rate of an domestic cover: 1- the distance to destination, 2- additional services, and 3- charity tax. In the next three sections we investigate each one of these in more detail. In the 4th section, I present items that do not conform with the rules provided in section 1 to 3.

1 - Distance

The basic rate of covers was determined based on the distance between the source and the destination:

  • Intra city: the destination city is the same as the sender's
  • Local: the destination city is less than 2 Farsakh (roughly 6.5km) away
  • Country: more than 2 Farsakh
Intra city

The rate of sending a cover to a destination within the same city was 2 Chahi for the whole era.

2 - Additional Services

There were other services available to the sender to request:

  • registration: The sender receives a receipt that the letter is delivered to the post office. [is there more to this?]
  • Advice of receipt (AR): a receipt to be sent to the original sender once the receiver receives the letter.
  • Insurance: A tax for insuring the letter for sending money.
registration

Insurance

This service provided insurance for when the letter contained money. The insurance was 1% of the total money being sent.

The cover is tied with 3ch Ahmad Shah portrait issue of 1911, 1kr Coat of Arms issue of 1909 with 1333 Hedgira overprint and 13ch Coat of Arms issue of 1909 revalued to 12ch. How is insurance calculated? 6 postage rate + 16ch registration + 6ch receipt + 6ch insurance(3T * 200 = 600ch total money-- > 1 % or 1ch is the insurance cost) + 1ch for wax"

3 - Charity Tax

During certain period, due to natural disaster, a charity was tax imposed for domestic letters in some cities.

Tehran

In February 1918, during the famine, Tehran made a 1 Chahi charity tax compulsory on domestic letters, postcards, parcel post and telegrams to raise funds for the poor; foreign letters were exempt. The tax was paid with the 1915 Coat of Arms revenue stamp handstamped "Masaref-e Kheirieh" (charity usage), and towards the end with ordinary postage stamps. Recorded usage runs from 9 March 1918 to 20 July 1919 (Behruz Nassre, The Charity Tax Issues of Tehran, 1918–1919, IPSC). His earlier exhibit gave 21 February 1918 to 26 May 1919.

Tehran charity type 1

Item 40024

A registered cover sent from Hamedan to Tehran, received on 10/1/19. The cover is franked with two Ahmad Shah portrait issue of 1911 and one 1ch charity stamp. The note potentially by the post masters shows the rate for the cover is 95 (dinar) equal to 19 Chahi.

See this cover in Censors

Tehran charity type 3

Racht

Rasht had its own 1 Chahi charity stamp for domestic letters from May 1918, during a typhoid epidemic and the occupation of the city by Mirza Kuchik Khan's Jangali movement. Recorded usage runs from 10 May 1918 to 2 July 1919; fewer than 20 covers are recorded (Behruz Nassre, Persia, The Charity Tax Issues of 1918–1921, IPSC).

No example in the collection yet.

Tabriz

In late October 1918, as soon as the Ottoman occupation forces left Tabriz, the city added a 1 Chahi tax on domestic letters (2 Chahi on parcel post and telegrams) to support its infirmaries and orphanages, paid with the "Infirmaries and Orphanages of Tabriz" stamps. Recorded usage runs from 28 October 1918 to 20 March 1921 (Behruz Nassre, Persia, The Charity Tax Issues of 1918–1921, IPSC).

1 Chahi

Item 40049

A cover sent from Tauris on 21/6/19 to Tehran, received on 25/6/19 franked with 6ch provisional issue of 1919 with 1ch charity stamp of Tabriz famine releif issue. This cover was also bears Dunterforce censorship mark(CP- 84).

See this cover in Censors
Khoy

For less than a month in March 1920, Khoy added a 1 Chahi tax on domestic letters for its infirmaries and orphanages. Only a single complete cover and fewer than 20 pieces are recorded (Behruz Nassre, Persia, The Charity Tax Issues of 1918–1921, IPSC).

No example in the collection yet.

2 - Special Premiums

Toward the end of the era, there was two more options available to senders on how to send their letters.

Overland Mail

Overland Mail was a service that would expediate delivery of parcels to Europe by carrying them in an armored vehicle through the desert from Baghdad to Haifa. Senders from Iran that wanted to use this service had to pay its premium.

Item 40172

Air Mail

The Basra-Cairo air route become available in 1922. Senders could pay the extra air fee for their letters to go to Basra and then go to Cairo by air and then get shipped to its final destination.

Before 1924

2 Kran

Item 40104

4 - Out of ordinary letters

Domestic covers routed through other countries

During the civil war and the Russian occupation of Tabriz, some of the letters From Tabriz were routed through Russia and the foreign rate applied to them despite being sent to a city in Iran.

Routed through Russia

Item 40127

A cover sent from Tauris on ?/9/12 to Meched, received on 21/9/12. Normally this cover would travel internally through Tehran with 6ch postal rate, however due to domestic turmoil in Iran it was sent through Russia with 13ch rate. The cover is tied with three 3ch and four 1ch Ahmad Shah portrait issue of 1911 with the Officiel handstamp.
